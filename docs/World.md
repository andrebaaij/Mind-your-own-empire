# The world of *Mind your own Empire*

A living lore document. Settled decisions are marked **Agreed**; anything marked **Proposal** is still a draft to argue with. Where you handed a decision to me, it's marked **Decided** and the reasoning is given, so it's easy to overrule.

> **On the source material.** This is fan work set in Iain M. Banks' Culture universe. The Culture, the Idirans and the Minds belong to the Banks estate. Everything specific to this game (the planet, its vanished owners, their caretakers, our Mind) is our own invention. That keeps a clean line if the project ever goes beyond a hobby.

---

## Premise — Agreed

The Idiran–Culture War. A Culture Mind called ***Only Visiting*** comes down alone on a planet whose builders Sublimed long ago and left their machines running. It has to make itself at home with what the ground provides, without upsetting the caretakers more than necessary, and before the Idirans find it.

It believes it is passing through. It is wrong about that, for reasons nobody has told it yet (see [The secret](#the-secret--agreed)).

## Era — Agreed: early in the war

The Idiran–Culture War: open war between the Culture and the Idiran Empire, fought across a huge stretch of space, mostly by ships. Minds are both the Culture's greatest asset and the Idirans' prime targets. To the Idirans, a Mind is an abomination: a machine that presumes to think. To the Culture, it's a colleague.

**We are early in the war.** The Culture went into it with almost no military, and it shows: it is losing ground, retooling for war, and evacuating people from the Idirans' path. A lone Mind on a contested world can expect no rescue, because there is nothing spare to send. That fits *Only Visiting* being truly alone, and gives the refugees a reason to exist.

## The Mind: *Only Visiting* — Agreed (name, voice, alone); Proposal (backstory)

**Voice: dry.** Understated, precise, quietly amused at its own predicament and everybody else's. It never panics in public. It is unfailingly polite to things that are trying to kill it. Banks' Minds are vastly more intelligent than anyone they talk to, and know it. The humour comes from restraint, not jokes.

**Proposal: backstory.** *Only Visiting* was the Mind of a small Contact ship, sent to survey the system and leave. The ship was caught by an Idiran patrol. The Mind ejected in its armoured core, and the rest of the ship was lost. It came down hard, with most of its sensors gone (hence the fog of war) and a small store of material and energy. Its first drones are built from the wreck.

**Agreed: truly alone.** No Culture help is coming for the Mind itself. What does arrive, once, is a supply pod (see [the campaign](#campaign--agreed-mission-based), mission 6), sent for reasons the Mind only understands later.

Its name was a joke when the ship was commissioned. It is less funny now, and that is exactly the kind of thing the Mind would point out.

**Sample lines (tone reference):**
- On landing: *"Arrived. Not, I'll concede, in the manner intended."*
- Starting the game: *"Sensors at four per cent. Drones at three. Optimism at a cautious nine."*
- Out of bloom: *"We appear to have built everything we can afford, and one thing we can't."*
- Can't build there: *"I could put it there. It would then fall over. Let's not."*
- First caretaker sighting: *"Someone's tidying up. Whoever it is, they've been at it for a while."*
- First Idiran contact: *"Ah. Company. The kind that brings its own opinions about me."*
- Unit idle: *"Awaiting instruction, or failing that, inspiration."*

## The planet: Lessuin — Decided

A rust-red, iron-rich world, once landscaped by its builders and now going slowly wild. The game's existing terrain maps onto it:

| In the game | In the world |
| --- | --- |
| Red iron-rich dirt | Ground saturated with metal from the builders' industry; the iron "grows" back slowly where caretakers still seed it |
| Bloom (orange crystal clusters) | Iron the builders grew rather than mined: feedstock the caretakers still cultivate, for manufacture that no longer happens. "Bloom" is the old smelters' word for raw iron; the Mind borrows it. |
| Acid pools in hollows | Spill basins from abandoned processing: still active, still dangerous |
| Ridges and cliffs | Eroded embankments and terraces of old works, half-returned to geology |
| Unexplored darkness | The Mind's ruined sensors; it can only see what its drones see |

**What it feels like to stand there.** It is always late afternoon on the Mind's side of Lessuin, and the air is warm and full of drifting spores. The primary hangs on the horizon: a banded, ringed gas giant, mostly in shadow. The wildlife is plants that took over the Tessilend's gardens:

- **Rust-grass**, red at the root and gold at the tips, on the fertile ground.
- **Copperleaf shrubs** with verdigris hearts.
- **Parasol spires**: thin trees with flat canopies that grow in groves.
- **Lantern stalks**, whose bulbs pulse teal in the hollows and on the sand.

Boulders gather where the old terraces broke into cliffs. Here and there a Tessilend column or arch is still standing, with nothing left to hold up. Lessuin's animals took the gardens over along with the plants. The Mind names each one the first time it sees it:

- **Trundlers:** slate-plated, six-legged grazers about the size of a drone, with amber eyes. They live in small herds with a youngster or two, and bolt from anything that hovers.
- **Kites:** manta-winged gliders the colour of the sunset, with a glowing tail lure. They circle over the groves, apparently for fun.
- **Longshanks:** a pale, banded body slung between four stilt legs, with a glowing lure on a tether. A longshank walks across the far country without hurrying and gives the base a wide berth. The Mind intends to be extremely polite to them.

Around the Mind there is a ring of scorched ground and scattered hull plating, from its landing. The acid pools still shimmer.

**Decided: Lessuin**, the builders' own name for it, which the Mind reads off a ten-thousand-year-old maintenance plaque in mission 2. On the Culture's charts it is *Survey object 31-Ledger-7*. The Mind uses the catalogue name exactly once, in mission 1, and never again: *"I refuse to be marooned somewhere with a hyphen in its name."*

## The Sublimed builders: the Tessilend — Decided

A civilization that reached the point where the material universe stopped being interesting and **Sublimed**: left it entirely, all at once, thousands of years ago. They did not take their machines, and did not switch them off.

**Decided: the Tessilend.** Fastidious, patient and long gone. They built for permanence and wrote everything down. The Mind calls them *the Landlords*, mostly when the caretakers are being difficult.

What they left behind:
- **Infrastructure** still half-working: power taps, fabrication beds, transit lines.
- **Relics** that are hard to interpret. These are the natural tech tree and the source of the game's mysteries.

### Sites and lattice — Decided, in the game

The Tessilend built in **lattice**, a green-blue crystal they grew rather than mined. It still grows in seams around their sites, wherever their machines go on feeding it. Lattice is the game's second resource, after bloom. It's scarce and slow to mine, and it's what you need for anything Tessilend: restoring their machines and building from their patterns. The Mind finds it hums, very faintly, in a key it doesn't recognise.

Every site stands on a paved apron, the Tessilend's **foundations**. The Custody treats anything built on them as built to plan, part of the estate, and never tidies it. That's the first house rule the Mind learns, in mission 3.

| Site | What studying it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Marker stone** | An inscription: a label, a notice, a line of history. The one in mission 2 gives the planet its name. |
| **Archive** | A fragment of the Tessilend story (in order: departure, census, guests, instructions, the open door), and a chart of the nearest sites not yet found |
| **Power tap** | Studied, then restored with 30 lattice: +8 power, permanently |
| **Fabrication bed** | Teaches a design. The first teaches the scout drone, which costs lattice. Later ones give salvaged lattice. |
| **Seedbed** | Woken, its lattice seams grow back after mining: the only renewable lattice |
| **Habitat** | Sealed. Lit, aired, swept, and empty. The Custody keeps house for someone, and won't say who. (It is for the refugees; mission 9 opens it.) |
- **Their caretakers.**

## The caretakers: the Custody — Agreed in outline; name Decided

Machines the builders left to keep the world as they liked it. They are not hostile so much as house-proud. They mend, tidy, prune and remove anything that doesn't belong, and as far as they are concerned that includes the Mind.

**Proposal: how they behave.**
- They follow ancient instructions, literally. They don't negotiate the way people do, but **they can be reasoned with through their own rules**. This is the "Contact" layer: the Mind can learn their protocols and find ways to be classed as allowed: a guest, a contractor, a tenant.
- Their response escalates if ignored: first they "tidy" (dismantle unattended buildings), then they evict.
- They are **not interested in the war**. That makes them dangerous to the Idirans too, and potentially a third party the Mind can turn to its advantage.

This gives the player two ways to deal with them: force, which is expensive and makes them escalate, or understanding, which is slower and unlocks relics. That choice is the most "Culture" part of the design.

**Decided: the Custody**, the Tessilend's word for the whole caretaking system. Its individual machines are **wardens**: slow, elegant, verdigris-bronze, each a floating ring trailing fine manipulators, with an amber working light. The Mind's private name for them is *"the staff"*.

**Decided: the first rule players learn.** Wardens dismantle buildings that are *unattended*: no drone close by and no sensor spire covering them. The Mind and the spires themselves are never touched; the Custody respects anything that is keeping watch. Mission 2 is built around that one rule. Later protocols (layout rules, tenancy) come in mission 3 and mission 9.

## The Idirans — Agreed

Canon: tall, three-legged, armoured, devout and formidable. The Culture's opponents in the war. They regard Minds as abominations.

**Proposal: why they come here.**
- To find and destroy the Mind (as in *Consider Phlebas*, where the Idirans hunt a Culture Mind across a world).
- And, once they realize what the planet is, to **claim the builders' relics** for the war effort.

**Proposal: how they arrive in game.** Scouting parties first, then a landing in force. The caretakers treat them as trespassers too, which is the Mind's opening.

## The secret — Agreed

**Humans are coming. The player doesn't know that at the start, and neither does the Mind.**

The original 2014 premise, a Mind "preparing the world for the coming of humans", survives, but as a reveal. *Only Visiting* was never only visiting.

**Agreed: refugees.** Culture people displaced by the Idiran advance, with nowhere safe left to go.

**Proposal: the details.**
- **Who:** the population of an Orbital in the Idirans' path, evacuated early in the war. They travel in a slow, overloaded flotilla, too many people and too few fast ships. **Decided: Kessenthal Orbital**, eleven billion people in the Idirans' path, who chose to run rather than be fought over.
- **Why here:** a world nobody else wants, far from the front, with a full planetary infrastructure that still works and machines that keep it habitable. The builders' caretakers have spent thousands of years maintaining a home for tenants who never came back.
- **Why the Mind wasn't told:** *Only Visiting* was sent to confirm the world was suitable, under sealed orders. If the Idirans caught a Mind that knew where the refugees were heading, the flotilla would be lost. So it was told it was only visiting. It was not.
- **How it's revealed:** the supply pod carries a key that unlocks the Mind's sealed orders. Before that, the player can pick up clues: caretakers preparing empty habitats, the Mind noticing memory it can't access, the pod's oddly specific cargo.

## Campaign — Agreed: mission-based

A campaign of missions in three acts. Each mission teaches or twists one idea, and the Mind's commentary carries the story between them. Mission names, objectives and twists below are a **proposal**.

### Act I: Landfall (learning to live here)

1. **Arrived.** The Mind wakes in its wrecked core with three drones built from the debris. *Objectives:* harvest bloom, build a sensor spire to restore some sight, raise a solar collector. *Teaches:* selection, orders, building, fog. *Closing line:* *"Sensors at four per cent. Drones at three. Optimism at a cautious nine."*
2. **The Staff.** *(Built.)* Caretakers appear and calmly dismantle a building left unattended. *Objectives:* watch a warden, read the marker stone it keeps straightening (which names Lessuin and the Tessilend), and hold on to your base. *Teaches:* caretakers as a third party that can't simply be shot, and studying sites.
3. **House Rules.** *(Built.)* The Mind reads a Tessilend archive and learns the first house rule: anything built on the builders' foundations is part of the estate. *Objectives:* study the archive, mine lattice, restore a power tap, build three things on the paving and get classed as a *contractor*, then find what the staff are tending in the east. *Teaches:* sites, lattice, foundations. *Clue:* a habitat, lights on, kept ready for ten thousand years.

### Act II: Company (the war arrives)

4. **Ah. Company.** *(Built.)* An Idiran scouting party lands, and its skimmers sweep the country around the crash. *Objectives:* run silent, plant a decoy beacon away from the base, lure a skimmer to it, then stay hidden until they give up. *Teaches:* stealth and detection: signatures (the Mind loudest, moving drones louder than idle ones), cover under the groves, running silent, decoys. Left alone, the Idirans narrow the search and triangulate on the Mind; a smashed decoy resets the search around itself. *Closing line:* *"Ah. Company: the kind that brings its own opinions about me."*
5. **Trespassers.** *(Built.)* The Idirans dig in around a Tessilend seedbed, three camps with a skimmer guarding each, and start stripping it. The Mind can't fight them, but the camps stand in the Custody's territory, and the staff tidy away anything left unattended. They have even less patience with Idiran camps than with the Mind's buildings. *Objectives:* find the camps, then lure each guard away with chains of decoys while a warden is near, so the staff evict all three before the seedbed is stripped (ten minutes). *Teaches:* using a third party; the caretakers are as hostile to Idirans as to anyone.
6. **Post.** *(Built.)* A Culture supply pod streaks in, fired from far away by the General Systems Vehicle *Quiet Word In Your Ear*. The GSV couldn't stop, and wouldn't have explained itself if it could. It lands far to the south-east, and the Idirans saw it too. *Objectives:* unload the pod with worker drones before the skimmers secure it (decoys slow them down), then bring the drones home unseen. *Reward:* bloom, lattice, drone patterns, habitat seed stock the Mind has no use for, and a sealed key it can't open.
7. **Relics.** *(Built.)* North-west of the base stands a Tessilend long-range transmitter. *Objectives:* study it, restore it with 60 lattice, then charge it for 45 seconds through an uplink the Mind wires in. The uplink is the loudest thing for miles while it charges, so skimmers converge on it and decoys have to keep them busy. *Twist:* the signal opens the sealed key from the pod.

### Act III: Arrivals (the secret)

8. **Sealed Orders.** *(Built.)* The key unlocks the Mind's own sealed memory: a refugee flotilla from Kessenthal Orbital is coming, and this world is where it is going. The Mind takes it about as well as a Mind can: *"I was told I was only visiting. In fairness, I was told a great many things."* *Objectives:* build three landing pads and keep them powered, and survey the two sealed habitats, all while a skimmer sweeps for the lit pads.
9. **Tenancy.** *(Built.)* The Custody will only open the habitats to rightful tenants. *Objectives:* read three Tessilend archives, under the staff's eye, until the planning records turn up the phrase "for guests"; then present the proof at the habitat, which the staff open: *"Guests. At last."* *Teaches:* winning without fighting.
10. **Only Visiting.** *(Built.)* The flotilla arrives, and so do Idiran raiders, who go for the landing pads. The Mind still can't fight, but the refugees are guests now and the staff look after their guests: wardens take hold of any skimmer they reach and see it off the planet. *Objectives:* land all eight ships, luring raiders towards the wardens with decoys and rebuilding wrecked pads. *Ending:* the Mind stays, and the world gets new tenants. **Decided, last line:** *"I did say I was only visiting. I never said for how long."*

### What the campaign needs from the game

In rough order of need, this is the feature roadmap:

- **A mission system:** objectives, triggers, scripted events, the Mind's commentary, win and lose conditions, and a mission select screen.
- **Caretakers:** units that maintain, tidy and evict. A protocol and reputation system per player.
- **Idirans:** scouts and heavier landing forces; a simple AI that searches, digs in and attacks.
- **Detection:** emissions and line of sight, so the Mind can hide.
- **Combat:** health, damage and repair for all three sides.
- **Story objects:** builders' facilities and relics, the supply pod, landing zones and habitats.

## Decisions you handed to me

- **Names:** Lessuin (planet), the Tessilend (builders), the Custody and its wardens (caretakers), Kessenthal Orbital (refugees). See the sections above.
- **Who fired the supply pod:** the GSV *Quiet Word In Your Ear*. A Mind back home that planned the refuge and knows more than it says. That leaves room for a sequel, and gives *Only Visiting* someone to be politely furious with.
- **Campaign size:** ten missions, built and released in acts, **plus a sandbox mode** ("Survey") for free play on any seed. Act I comes first.
- **Losing:** losing the Mind's core fails the mission, and you retry from its start. The campaign only remembers which missions you've completed.
- **Last line:** *"I did say I was only visiting. I never said for how long."*
